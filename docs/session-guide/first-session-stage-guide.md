# First Session — Stage-by-Stage Delivery Guide
Kappasinian first consultation, synthesized from three sources: the First Consultation workbook (201-9), the PSR script practice guide (mentor: Denise Delahoussaye), and a class session recording. All wording original. Delivery notes in parentheses.

**Delivery legend**
- (slow) / (very slow) — hypnotic pacing, ~85–92% conversational rate
- (voice ↓) — pitch and volume drop at the end of the phrase; suggestion lands harder
- (voice ↑) — brighten and lift; used for emergence and rapport moments
- (paternal) — direct, authoritative, declarative; default for Physical/literal clients
- (maternal) — warm, permissive, inviting; default for Emotional/inferred clients and challenge-adjacent moments
- (pause Ns) — silent beat of roughly N seconds
- (WAIT) — hard stop; do not continue until the client responds (nod, shake, finger lift, or verbal)
- (LISTEN) — client is talking; reflect before moving on
- (WATCH) — eyes on the client's body: breath depth, eyelid flutter, swallow, arm micro-movements, head drop
- (SNAP) — finger snap immediately before "Deep sleep"
- (E-swap) — for Emotional/inferred clients, soften the line: "allowing," "tendency," "you might notice," "giving yourself permission," "because you choose to"
- (P-keep) — for Physical/literal clients, keep it direct and literal: "your hand IS lighter," black-and-white cause-effect

---

## 0. Setup & Presenting Issue (≈10–15 min)

### 0.1 Environment and physical prep
- Client seated upright, feet flat, back against the chair (verify by asking for one or two small position adjustments — this doubles as an early compliance check) (conversational pace)
- Glasses off, gum out, phone silenced
- Non-dominant arm resting on the desk or table; other hand on the thigh
- Ask whether sustained eye contact is comfortable for them (WAIT) (some clients find it confronting — offer the option before requiring it)

### 0.2 Presenting-issue conversation
- Open with L.O.V.E.: Listen, Observe, Verify, Empathize (LISTEN throughout; reflect their exact keywords back — their words, not yours)
- Unfiltered awareness: interpret their descriptions from *their* life experience, never through your own filter (note-take their literal phrases for reuse in stage 8)
- Open-ended questions first — invite the story: when it began, what it costs them, what they've tried (LISTEN) (conversational, warm)
- Closed-ended questions second — anchor each realization the client reaches with a yes/no ("So the pattern really took hold after ___, is that right?") (WAIT for the yes) (each anchored yes adjusts the critical filter before hypnosis ever begins)
- Preparation of the critical mind — by the end of intake the client should be able to answer four things in their own head:
  1. What the problem is
  2. Why they have it
  3. What needs to change
  4. How we are going to change it
- Pillars: identify 2–3 cognitive supports you will build conversationally now so the hypnotic suggestions later land on prepared ground (e.g., "you already have the skill; only the alarm is outdated")
- Thinking ahead: whatever you plan to use in trance — a calm scene, a reset cue, a comfort dial — elicit or pre-frame it HERE, cognitively, before induction (if doing desensitization later, have them pick their special calm place now)
- (Booking-gate check: referral-lane issues require the written referral already on file; minors require guardian consent, children guardian presence)

## 1. Pre-Induction Speech (≈3 min)
Purpose: dispel myths, address fears, set expectations. (conversational → gradually slower; paternal baseline, maternal for E-clients)

- Hypnosis is a natural, everyday state — the drift before sleep and just after waking, highway miles you don't remember, being absorbed in a film and not hearing your name (give 2–3 everyday examples; pick ones that match their life)
- It is a narrow, comfortable focus that lets the mind's protective filter relax so a negative pattern can be exchanged for a positive one
- "Deep sleep" is a cue phrase for this relaxed state — not actual sleep (voice ↓ on "deep sleep" even here; you are pre-loading the anchor)
- Expectations, stated plainly:
  - You stay awake and aware — possibly *more* aware; hearing can extend to small sounds in the room
  - You hear everything; you stay in control; you cannot get stuck
  - You would never do or say anything you wouldn't do wide awake — not a truth serum
  - Thoughts of the day will drift through; the mind may even debate whether it's hypnotized — that wondering is usually the sign that it IS (smile; this lands as reassurance)
  - You could open your eyes and get up — you'll simply prefer not to, because it feels good to stay relaxed
  - Some feel light and tingly, some pleasantly heavy, some just calm — whatever you feel is right for you
- Do-with, not do-to: their participation and imagination are the engine (this pre-frames cooperation)
- Bridge to their issue: "the reason you're here is ___, and hypnosis lets us work with the subconscious directly, so the change gets much easier" (hope + authority — they must leave this stage believing it will work for THEM)
- Frame the testing: "I'll run a few tests to learn how you take in information — no passing, no failing, so no helping and no hindering" (paternal, light)
- "Any questions before we begin?" (WAIT) (if none: "Good — because some people think..." and pre-empt one common worry; if questions: answer briefly, don't reopen the whole speech)

## 2. Theory of Mind (≈5 min)
Draw it while talking — a visual construct of how the change will happen. (teaching cadence; check-ins throughout)

- 2.1 Primitive area — base of the mind, inherited survival instinct; two settings: fight (push through stress) and flight (withdraw/escape) ("Ever heard of fight-or-flight?") (WAIT for nod)
- 2.2 Birth to ~age eight — highly suggestible; nearly everything accepted as absolute truth; learning happens through positive and negative association (use a food example — loving sweets, refusing vegetables — and ask which side THEY were on as a kid) (LISTEN; small rapport beat)
- 2.3 Knowns / subconscious — those associations become the life script, the familiar; this is the subconscious mind, ~88% of total mind power (write "88%")
- 2.4 Critical mind — forms around eight; the filter that guards the knowns, even when the known IS the problem ("that's when kids start asking *why* do I have to do it that way")
- 2.5 Conscious mind — logic, reason, decision-making, analysis, willpower — "the willpower that never works when you actually need it" (small laugh beat) — only ~12%
- 2.6 Run THEIR issue through the model (this is the pivot — personalize completely):
  - The conscious 12% decides to change → the thought travels down → hits the critical filter → gets checked against the old programming → the programming resists → the filter blocks it → the same pattern runs again (trace it on the drawing with your finger, slow)
  - Name what their subconscious currently holds as "known" (e.g., irregular sleep, alarm at group attention) — their familiar, however unwanted
- 2.7 What hypnosis does: takes the same intention past the filter, straight to the subconscious, and installs the new pattern with positive suggestion; with repetition and reinforcement the new becomes the familiar
- 2.8 The contract line: "I'll work with the 88, you work with the 12 — together, one hundred percent, all moving toward ___" (their goal, their words) (voice ↑, energizing) ("How does that sound?") (WAIT for the yes — this is a critical anchored yes)
- 2.9 Bridge: "And the way we start is by testing your suggestibility."

## 3. Suggestibility Testing (≈5 min)

### 3.1 Frame the rules (paternal, precise)
- Position re-check: upright, feet flat, arm on the desk, other hand on thigh (make one or two micro-adjustments and watch them comply — instruction-following check)
- All answers non-verbal: nod = yes, shake = no, look directly at me = "I don't know / find the answer for me"
- Repeat: not pass/fail; no helping, no hindering
- Roadmap one step ahead only: "after the testing we'll convert to hypnosis right here in this chair, then move you to the recliner, relax you deeply, and give you the suggestions" (reduces unknowns = fewer message units spent on anxiety)

### 3.2 Visualization pre-check
- "Look at your arm from the elbow to the fingertips — take a good picture of it — and close your eyes. See that arm and hand in your mind; when you can remember what they look like, nod." (WAIT) (this is both a test and the induction's first step)

### 3.3 The questions (full 36 in a real first session; short set for PSR practice)
Deliver evenly, no leading tone; note answers mentally, especially the last two. (steady rhythm; ~4s WAIT after each)
- Ever walked or talked in your sleep? (WAIT)
- Ever woken at night unable to move or speak for a moment? (WAIT)
- After an argument, do you replay what you could have said? (WAIT)
- Thinking of biting a sour lemon — does your mouth water? (WAIT) (WATCH: an actual swallow here is a strong physical-suggestibility tell)
- Can you picture things clearly in your mind? (WAIT) (if no → "Can you *remember* what images look like?" — imagination fallback)
- (Full battery also samples control fears, heights, closed spaces, emotional expression to parents/partner — E-indicators — score to physical% / emotional%; the split drives every wording choice from here on)

## 4. Physiological Changes (≈2 min)
Purpose: convincers + message-unit build. (slowing now; voice settling ↓; WATCH constantly — you will see each change before they report it)

- Pre-frame: "As you begin to enter hypnosis, your body will show a series of changes. I'll notice them before you do — when YOU become aware of each one, nod and let me know."
- Breathing deepens as the body takes in more oxygen — "when you notice, nod" (WAIT) (WATCH the chest; often you can time your line to their actual inhale)
- Dryness in lips, mouth, throat; urge to swallow — "completely natural — nod when you feel it" (WAIT) (WATCH the throat)
- Eye flutter — "notice the eyes now... that faint fluttering beneath the lids" (pause 1s) "...the first stage of hypnosis" (voice ↓) (no WAIT needed — declare it)

---

## 5. Arm-Raising Conversion (≈8–10 min)
The primary induction and heart of the first session. Branch entirely on suggestibility mode. (WATCH the hand continuously; pace your lines to what the arm is actually doing, never ahead of it) (full stage-by-stage detail: `arm-raising-playbook.md`)

### 5.0 Mechanics before you start (P3)
- Guide them into position (you direct, they follow — an early compliance check); working arm and hand ON the table, other hand on the thigh; glasses off, no gum
- Pre-check the movement: have them raise the hand to the forehead once — you are checking the mechanics AND inferring the arc the hand will travel (quietly rehearses the target)
- Take away verbal responses now — answers are non-verbal from here (removing speech adds overload → more message units)
- Last suggestibility question must be "can you visualize?" — if no, downshift to "imagine" or "conceptualize" so no one fails
- (WATCH the RISE for the E/P tell: a Physical Suggestible's hand rises led by the **wrist**; an Emotional Suggestible's rises straighter **out** from the arm)

### 5.1 Literal / Physical branch (P-keep; paternal; rhythmic, driving)
- Attention path: elbow pressing into the table → forearm → wrist → hand → fingertips (focus the wrist — a specific physical area)
- Direct declarations from the start: "lift and rise, lighter and lighter" — light as a feather, then lighter than that (repeat in waves; sync "lighter" to their inhales)
- Announce the pulling/jerking motions as fact (small twitches WILL appear; naming them as they happen is the convincer loop)
- Misdirection block (handles message-unit overload): the OTHER hand grows heavier against the leg... feet press into the floor... back against the chair (alternate light-hand / heavy-hand / feet / breath — rotation keeps the load high without repetition fatigue)
- Checkpoint: "when you feel that difference in weight, nod" (WAIT)
- Escalate ONLY once the hand actually moves off the surface: "twist and turn" — palm and fingertips inward, upward and inward, drawn to the face (twisting/turning makes it mechanically easier for the hand to reach the forehead; rhythmic, every breath = one increment)
- Head-to-hand close (P3): suggest the head grows heavy and drops down to meet the rising hand — closing the gap from both ends (WATCH: steer the hand toward the **forehead/brow, NOT the eyes** — avoid fingers poking into the eyes)
- Bicep cue: "the instant you feel the bicep tighten, nod" (WAIT)
- Re-announce dryness/salivation mid-climb (recycles an earlier convincer)

### 5.2 Inferred / Emotional branch (E-swap; maternal; softer, spacious, more pauses)
- Same attention path, permissive frame: "let your attention drift..." "you might imagine the hand becoming lighter..."
- Imagery carry: many powerful helium balloons tied around the wrist (imagery does the lifting for inferential learners)
- Control-preserving language throughout: "you're creating that sensation — and that gives you a greater sense of control," "because you choose to allow it," "giving yourself permission" (E-clients must feel in control or they resist)
- Same misdirection rotation (feet, back, breath, heavy hand) (softer, listing quality)
- Checkpoint: "when you notice the two hands feeling different, you can nod" (WAIT)
- Double-bind on approach: "I wonder whether it feels more like the hand drawn to the face, or the face to the hand, or both drawn together... I'll let you decide" (either answer = deeper compliance)

### 5.3 Peak and conversion (both branches)
- "When the hand comes all the way in to touch the face — the moment you make skin contact — nod, and you'll reach your peak of suggestibility and enter the hypnotic sleep." (WAIT for contact + nod)
- (SNAP) "Deep sleep." (voice ↓↓, strong)

### 5.4 Post-hypnotic suggestion to re-hypnosis — REQUIRED here and after EVERY deepener in a first session
Full anatomy, never abbreviated:
- cue words ("deep sleep") + purpose clause ("for the purpose of hypnosis") + consent clause ("and with your permission") + speed/quality triple ("quickly, calmly, and deeply") + somatic tag ("and the physical body relaxes") (measured, formal cadence — this is the contract)
- Say the peak-"deep sleep" + PHS pair **6–8 times across the first session** (once at the peak, once after each deepener) — this is what makes the second-session "deep sleep" drop them straight back in (Law of Association + Law of Repetition)

### 5.5 The depth ladder (P3) — read the eyes under the lids after the escape
Name the depth silently; it tells you how far the work can go this session.
- **Hypnoidal** — eyes up-and-down, quick (REM-like): first depth
- **Cataleptic** — eyes side-to-side, more fluid, slower: middle depth
- **Somnambulism** — eyes roll up, whites may show (like a sleeping child): deepest, three stages, each with increasing *post-session* amnesia (Stage 1 ≈ 30%, Stage 2 ≈ 60%, Stage 3 ≈ 80%+)
- The client is conscious and in control throughout — the amnesia is *afterward*, like fading dream-recall. Everyone can reach somnambulism; a ≈50/50 Somnambulist reaches it most easily. An Emotional Suggestible may be **deep yet report feeling light** (lower ego sensation) — trust the eye signs over their self-report. Go only as deep as the goal needs.

## 6. Count 5 → 0 Deepener (≈1 min)
- "Gently relax the hand, let it come back down." (pause 1s)
- Frame: each count carries you deeper
- 5 — going down now, deeper into hypnosis... 4 — physically releasing, relaxing, letting go... 3 — an emotional calmness... 2 — mentally relaxing now... 1 — deeper and deeper... (each number: voice ↓ a step; ~1s gap)
- "And zero —" (SNAP) "Deep sleep."
- Repeat PHS (full anatomy)

---

## 7. Transfer + Deepeners (≈5 min)
Move client to the recliner between the chair phase and deepening (guide by the arm, minimal words, keep the voice low and continuous — the walk itself is a deepener if you narrate calm). Then stack 2–3 of:

### 7.1 Reactional (eye-open/close)
- Pre-frame: "In a moment — not yet — you'll open your eyes and fix them on a spot above you; when I say deep sleep they close and you go twice as deep" ("not yet" prevents premature opening)
- "Open your eyes" (WAIT — can take a few seconds) → (SNAP) "Deep sleep — twice as deep" (repeat cycle ×4; alternate with/without "twice as deep")
- Repeat PHS (first session)

### 7.2 Heavy/Light (arms out)
- Eyes open, arms out at shoulder height (demonstrate with your own arms); one palm up, other hand sideways thumb up; eyes close
- Direct load on the palm: a heavy book pressing down; inferred pull on the thumb: many helium balloons (then SILENCE — WATCH the arms respond for several seconds; the silence is the test)
- "Good — release both ideas, arms gently to the lap, and —" (SNAP) "Deep sleep."
- Repeat PHS (first session)
- (Age swap: skip for elders — fall/mobility imagery risk)

### 7.3 Staircase / imagery deepener (style by age band)
- Adult: staircase, count 10→0 (or 20→0 vintage-style for image work, below), each step twice as deep (slow, even, voice stepping ↓)
- Child: gentle slide with pillows at the bottom, count 3→0 (playful lexicon)
- Elder: level garden path, stepping stones, bench at the end (no descent/fall imagery)
- Vintage ideomotor image-work variant (from the class recording — powerful for self-image issues):
  - Client visualizes themself at the top of a 20-step staircase with a secure handrail, seen as if in a mirror
  - Left index finger lifts when the image is formed (WAIT for finger) ("the finger lifts in little jerking motions, and it will go down the same way")
  - Right index finger lifts if they DESIRE to change the image (WAIT) (a lifted finger = subconscious consent to change)
  - Have them mold the new image — confidence, self-liking, whatever they choose to add or subtract (permissive list, maternal)
  - Descend 20→0; each count = the new image fixing deeper into memory; the mind works toward this image subconsciously (very slow count; sparse words between numbers)
  - (WATCH for spontaneous head-turn/jerk — from the recording: acknowledge internally as a depth indicator, tell the client to pay it no attention)

## 8. Progressive Relaxation (≈3–4 min)
(This is typically where the recording for the client starts, if recording. Slowest pacing of the session; theta-adjacent tone; long pauses)

- Opening: rest back, feel the rhythm of the breath, giving yourself permission to let me guide you (maternal, regardless of E/P — everyone gets warmth here)
- Sound reframe (insert early): outside sounds are only the everyday sounds of life — they cannot distract or disturb; they simply relax you deeper (inoculates against every hallway noise for the rest of the session)
- Top-down wave: scalp → forehead → temples → eyelids → cheeks → jaw (400–800ms beats between body parts)
- → neck → shoulders (softening) → upper arms → elbows → forearms → wrists → hands → fingertips
- → chest (opening, releasing what no longer serves) → solar plexus → belly (unwinding)
- Breath checkpoint: deep, gentle, rhythmic — the drowsy, daydreamy feeling taking over — letting go, drifting down (voice at its lowest)
- → hips → thighs (large muscles and small) → knees → shins → calves → ankles → heels → arches → every toe to the tips (optionally: a tingling)
- Close with 5→0: each count deeper — five, letting go... four... three... two... one... zero — (SNAP) "Deep sleep."
- Repeat PHS (first session)
- (VAK note: lead the opening with the client's rep system — visual client: "see a soft light of calm at the crown"; auditory: "hear how quiet the breath has become"; kinesthetic: "feel the first wave of softening" — then weave the other two)

## 9. Therapeutic Suggestions (≈5 min)
Formula: **Say it → See it → Feel it.**

### 9.1 Say it (words matched to mode)
- Literal (P): direct present/progressive declarations — the behavior IS changing, cause-effect, concrete ("one slow breath before the first word switches the body to calm — every time") (paternal, even, certain; voice ↓ on the operative verb)
- Inferred (E): permissive invitations — "you might notice...", "imagine how it feels to...", "perhaps...", "it can be interesting to discover..." (maternal, spacious; longer pauses so their imagination fills the gaps)
- Blended (40–60%): open inferred, land literal
- No-rush cadence (either mode): there's no rush... it's unfolding now... naturally... automatically... with each breath... (fragment lines, long pauses — this IS the delivery, not filler)
- Identity close: you're not becoming someone new — you're remembering who you've always been (voice ↓, slow, final)
- Embed the pillars built in stage 0; reuse their exact keywords from intake

### 9.2 See it
- Place them in their pre-elicited scene (the one chosen BEFORE induction — thinking ahead pays off here); watch themselves living the change (visual client: rich scene detail; use their own best-memory imagery verbatim from intake)

### 9.3 Feel it
- Body-register the payoff: P-relationship framing = quick, visible-win satisfaction and momentum; E-relationship framing = deep private steadiness and lasting certainty (match to their relationship/suggestibility profile)

### 9.4 Situational rehearsal (vintage pattern, for relational/identity work)
- Walk them through 2–3 real upcoming situations; desensitize the anxiety, rehearse the logical, confident response; affirm options and self-worth ("you expect to be treated well") (calm, steady; pause after each scene to let it run)

## 10. Venting Dream Suggestion (optional, ≈30s)
- Tonight and in the nights ahead, dreams may quietly sort and release leftover tension about ___, so mornings arrive lighter (soft, almost throwaway placement right before emergence — it lands on the way out)

## 11. Count Out 0 → 5 (≈1 min)
(Reverse the energy curve: start low, end bright)
- Frame: coming back up calm and refreshed; each count more alert, more awake, positive sense of well-being
- 0 — shut the door to the subconscious, sealing out every negative (still low)
- 1 — slowly and calmly coming up (slightly ↑)
- 2 — physically aware, mentally alert (↑)
- 3 — coming up higher now (↑)
- 4 — eyes beginning to open, and — (↑, brightening)
- 5 — EYES OPEN, WIDE AWAKE (voice ↑↑, crisp, energetic)
- Rapid confirm: "one-two-three-four-five — eyes open, wide awake" (brisk)

## 12. Finger-Spread — PHS Verification (≈3–4 min)
Tests that the re-hypnosis suggestion took; also the default secondary induction for all subsequent sessions.

- Setup: feet flat, hands on legs; raise one arm bent at the elbow, palm ~10 inches from the face, fingers straight, just above eye level
- "Stare at the middle finger. Deep breath — and on the exhale, the fingers begin to separate."
- Repetition wave: separating... wider and wider... further and further apart (repeat 3–8× as needed; steady chant-like rhythm) (WATCH the fingers — narrate what actually happens)
- Fork question: "is it the hand, or the arm, pulling in toward your face?" (WAIT for answer — then use THEIR word, hand or arm, for the rest)
- Physiological recycle: breathing deepening — nod (WAIT); eyelids blinking, heavier — nod (WAIT) — "the more they blink the heavier they become, the heavier they become the more they blink... they'd much rather stay closed" (loop language, voice ↓)
- Contact: "when the ___ touches the face, nod — skin contact, eyes close, peak of suggestibility, hypnotic sleep" (WAIT) → (SNAP) "Deep sleep."
- Brief hold, then count out 0→5 again (full sequence, ending bright)

---

## 13. Homework + Close (≈3 min)
- Assign 1–2 concrete daily practices tied to the suggestions (e.g., twice daily one-minute cue + scene replay; one low-stakes real-world rep per day, logged) (conversational, collaborative — they should repeat it back)
- Future-pace: "picture next week — you walking in, telling me the first win" (voice ↑, warm)
- Book the next session before they leave

---

## 14. Subsequent Sessions — Delta
- Open with the week review — expect onion-peeling; each week reveals the issue more precisely (LISTEN; reflective listening so they feel heard; note keywords and body language)
- Open-ended → discovery → anchor with closed-ended yes (same critical-mind adjustment loop as session one)
- Think ahead DURING the review: choose tools and pre-frame anything the trance work will need
- Diagnostic tools as indicated: corrective therapy, pendulum, dream therapy, Paris window
- Protect a ~20-minute window for the hypnosis itself
- Induction: finger-spread conversion (no arm raising after session one) → deepener → PR → suggestions → count out → homework
- Standing rule, both modes, always: **Physicals need to FEEL hypnosis (body convincers); Emotionals need to feel UNDERSTOOD (rapport, imagery, control language).**

## Appendix C — PHS Anatomy (memorize as one unit)
cue words → "for the purpose of hypnosis" → "with your permission" → "quickly, calmly, and deeply" → "and the physical body relaxes"
Placement: after the first conversion and after EVERY deepener in a first session; the finger-spread in stage 12 is its live test. Delivered 6–8× total across the first session.

## Appendix D — Laws of Suggestibility (P3)
Why the repetition, the voice choices, and the challenges work.
1. **Association** — the client links your voice, the room, and the process to the hypnotic state; "deep sleep" comes to mean that calm (this is what the peak-anchor exploits).
2. **Repetition** — repeating suggestions within and across sessions strengthens them; repeat the *ways* you hypnotize too (why "deep sleep" + PHS is given 6–8× in session one).
3. **Delayed action** — strong in Emotional and Intellectual Suggestibles: (a) a goal tied to a future date manifests then (desensitized flight fear pays off on the trip); (b) inference can take a week or two to land — a Physical may recall a dream in 1–2 days, an Emotional in 5–6.
4. **Dominance** — default maternal/lulling voice, but paternal/dominant is right for challenges and for emphasis words in a suggestion. You are the expert in the room — natural one-upmanship, never domineering.
5. **Reverse action** — given a two-part suggestion, the client follows the *stronger* part. "You can try to open your eyes, but the harder you try the more they stay shut — you cannot open them": "cannot" beats "try" (deepener + convincer; if they do open, "Good — you passed the test!" and move on). Also re-associates a trigger: get them calm, challenge them to re-create the anxiety, and calm wins — installing calm where the trigger used to fire.

## Appendix E — Deepener refinements (P3)
- **5→0 countdown** — a good first deepener straight off the conversion.
- **Reactional** — moving the client to a recliner is itself a longer-form reactional deepener.
- **Arm rigidity** — if in person and the arm is not actually stiff, do NOT challenge the bend; just release the tension.
- **Eye fascination** — the key is to remove head movement so the eyes close naturally as the gaze/object lowers (doubles as a secondary induction).
- **Heavy/Light** — palm-up "heavy bag of sand" (literal) vs thumb-up "helium balloons" (inferential); also a suggestibility test (watch which hand responds more).
- **Staircase/Path** — check comfort first (finger-raise if safe); higher Physical Suggestible can rock gently as they "go down the stairs."
- **Focus on breathing / Focus on sounds** — "inhaling relaxation, exhaling tension"; "everyday sounds only send you deeper" (inoculates against room noise).
- **Progressive relaxation** — usually LAST; seated upright → head-down, reclined → feet-up; P responds to "relax," E to "calm."
