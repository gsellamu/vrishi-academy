## About This Guidebook

This guidebook is the companion to the Clinical Hypnotherapy Session Guidebook,
focused specifically on the **inferred (emotional)** lane of Kappasinian hypnotherapy.
Where the general guidebook covers both physical/literal and emotional/inferred
techniques, this one goes deep on the permissive, indirect approach — the language
patterns, voice modulations, and therapeutic strategies that work best with
emotionally suggestible clients.

All wording is original. Nothing is reproduced verbatim from HMI or Panorama
copyrighted workbooks. Concepts are grounded in Kappas's suggestibility theory,
Milton Erickson's language patterns, and HMI clinical training (101-series, 201-series).

## Part 1: Understanding the Emotional/Inferred Client

### 1.1 Suggestibility Spectrum

Kappas identified suggestibility as existing on a spectrum:

| Type | Physical % | Style | Responds to | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Physical (Literal)** | 60%+ | Direct, commanding | "It IS lifting" | To **feel** hypnosis (body phenomena) |
| **Emotional (Inferred)** | 40% or less | Permissive, indirect | "You might notice..." | To **feel understood** (rapport, imagery) |
| **Balanced (Blended)** | 40-60% | Open inferred, convert literal at deepening | Both styles | Flexibility from the therapist |

**Key insight:** The emotional client does not resist hypnosis — they resist being
told what to do. They enter trance through *permission*, not command. The same words
that deepen a physical client ("your hand IS lifting NOW") can stiffen an emotional
client's critical mind.

### 1.2 The E/P Tell

During the arm-raising induction, the therapist reads the **rise pattern**:

- **Physical suggestible:** Hand rises led by the **wrist** (mechanical, direct response)
- **Emotional suggestible:** Hand rises **straighter out** from the arm (inferential, whole-arm response)

This tells you which language lane to use for the rest of the session.

### 1.3 Emotional Client Characteristics

- **Learns:** Inferentially (reads between the lines)
- **Responds to:** Rapport, imagery, metaphor, permission
- **Ego sensations:** Lower (less body-phenomenon awareness during trance)
- **May appear:** Deep in trance yet report feeling "normal" or "light"
- **Trust the eyes:** Depth read from eye signs under lids, not from their self-report
- **E-override rule:** Paternal body-command stages (physio, conversion, eye fascination,
  heavy/light, finger spread) shift to **maternal** voice. Reactional shifts to **paternal**.

### 1.4 The Axis to Hold

Throughout the entire session with an emotional client, hold this axis:

> **Physical clients need to FEEL hypnosis.**
> **Emotional clients need to feel UNDERSTOOD.**

Every word choice, every tonal shift, every pause serves this axis.

### 1.5 Pre-Induction Speech for Emotional Clients

The pre-induction talk differs from the standard version. Where the physical client needs
to hear "you will feel relaxation," the emotional client needs to hear "you will stay in
control and you will understand what's happening." Frame the entire experience as
collaborative and familiar.

Key elements to include:
- **Awareness:** "You will not only be aware of my voice, but you will also hear sounds
  around you that seem louder than usual."
- **Normalization:** "Many thoughts of the day will pass through your mind, even questions
  about whether you are actually hypnotized."
- **Body sensations:** "You may feel some lightness, heaviness, or tingling."
- **Control frame:** "Even though these thoughts enter your mind, you will lose the desire
  to fight the suggestions because you really won't want to open your eyes or move."
- **Do-with language:** "This is a 'do with' process, not a 'do to' process."

This speech builds the message units needed for the induction while satisfying the
emotional client's need to feel informed and in control.

## Part 2: Inferred Language Patterns

### 2.1 The Permissive Vocabulary

Replace direct commands with permissive alternatives:

| Direct (Physical) | Permissive (Inferred) |
|---|---|
| Your hand IS getting lighter | You might imagine that hand becoming lighter |
| Relax now | You may find yourself beginning to relax |
| Close your eyes | When you are ready, you can let them close |
| Go deeper | Perhaps drifting a little deeper |
| You feel comfortable | You may notice a sense of comfort |
| It lifts now | There may be a tendency to lift |
| Sleep deeply | You can allow yourself to drift |

**Core permissive words:** allow, permit, imagine, drift, float, notice, gentle, soft,
warm, calm, easy, willing, choose, let, tendency, perhaps, peaceful, safe.

### 2.2 Presuppositions

Presuppositions assume the change is already happening. The client's critical mind
processes the surface structure while the embedded assumption bypasses the filter.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Temporal** | "As you begin to relax..." (assumes relaxation is starting) |
| **Awareness** | "When you notice the heaviness..." (assumes heaviness exists) |
| **Ordinal** | "The moment you feel that comfort..." (assumes comfort will arrive) |
| **Adverbial** | "While your body continues to release..." (assumes releasing is underway) |
| **Discovery** | "Before you realize how deep you are..." (assumes depth is coming) |
| **Wonder** | "You might already notice..." (assumes something is happening) |

**Delivery:** Smooth flow, stated as fact, no pause around the presupposition itself.
The assumption is baked into the grammar, not emphasized.

### 2.3 Embedded Commands

An embedded command is an imperative disguised within a longer, permissive sentence.
The command is marked with a subtle voice shift (lower pitch, slight pause before/after,
increased certainty) so the subconscious registers it while the conscious mind hears
the surrounding permissive frame.

**Structure:** Permissive wrapper + **EMBEDDED COMMAND** + continuation

Examples:
- "And I wonder if you can **FEEL THAT COMFORT** spreading through your body."
- "Many people find that they can **LET GO** more easily than they expected."
- "You might notice how good it feels to **RELAX NOW** and simply be here."
- "Perhaps you can begin to **TRUST YOURSELF** in this process."

**Analog marking (voice):** Drop pitch 5-10%, slow rate slightly, add micro-pause
before and after. The tonal shift marks the command for the subconscious. In SSML,
these are rendered with `<emphasis level="strong">`.

### 2.4 Double Binds

A double bind offers the illusion of choice where all options lead to the therapeutic
outcome. The emotional client's need for control is satisfied because they are choosing.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Classic** | "I wonder whether the hand is drawn to the face, or the face to the hand, or both drawn together. I will let you decide." |
| **Temporal** | "You can let this change happen quickly, or you can allow it to unfold gradually — either way, it arrives." |
| **Awareness** | "You may notice the comfort in this moment, or you may not notice it until later — but it is there." |
| **Process** | "Whether you drift into this state with the next breath or the one after, you will find yourself there." |

### 2.5 Truism Chains (Yes-Set → Lead)

Build a yes-set with three verifiable truths, then ride the momentum into a suggestion:

1. "You are sitting in that chair." *(true)*
2. "You can hear the sound of my voice." *(true)*
3. "Your breathing is finding its own rhythm." *(true)*
4. "**And that means** the part of you that wants this change is already at work." *(lead)*

The connector ("and that means," "which allows," "so you can begin to") bridges the
verified reality into the therapeutic suggestion.

### 2.6 Tag Questions

Lock in acceptance with a tag question that assumes agreement:

- "That feels comfortable, doesn't it?"
- "You can notice the difference, can't you?"
- "That makes sense, doesn't it?"

**Delivery:** Soften the voice on the tag — it's an invitation, not a challenge.

### 2.7 Milton Model Patterns (Summary)

| Pattern | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Permissive frame | Bypass resistance | "You might find..." |
| Presupposition | Assume the outcome | "As you continue to relax..." |
| Embedded command | Direct the subconscious | "...begin to FEEL THAT CALM..." |
| Double bind | Satisfy control need | "Quickly or gradually..." |
| Truism chain | Build yes-momentum | Three truths + lead |
| Tag question | Lock acceptance | "...doesn't it?" |
| Ambiguity | Widen interpretation | "And you can really let yourself go" |
| Metaphor | Bypass the filter | Isomorphic narrative |
| Withholding | Build anticipation | "But not just yet..." |

## Part 3: Inferred Inductions

### 3.1 Permissive Arm-Raising Induction

The arm-raising induction is the standard Kappasinian primary induction. For emotional
clients, the conversion phase uses permissive language instead of direct commands.

**Setup** (same for both lanes):
- Upright, feet flat, chosen arm on the table
- "Look at that arm, elbow to fingertips — take a good picture — and close your eyes."
- "When you can remember exactly what they look like, nod." **[WAIT]**

**Suggestibility questions** (same for both lanes):
- Sleep-walk/talk? Unable to move on waking? Replay arguments? Lemon mouth? Visualize?
- Weight the last two; if "no" to visualize, downshift to "imagine/conceptualize."

**Physiology nods** (same for both lanes):
- Breathing deepens → nod
- Lips/mouth/throat dry, urge to swallow → nod
- Eye flutter beneath the lids

**Conversion — Inferred Lane (maternal, permissive):**

> Let your attention drift to the elbow resting comfortably on the table, down through
> the forearm, into the wrist, the hand, the fingertips.
>
> And you might imagine that hand becoming lighter — as if many powerful helium balloons
> were tied around the wrist — light as a feather, and then even lighter.
>
> As you allow that, you may notice little pulling or tugging motions beginning — you are
> creating that sensation, and that gives you a greater sense of control.
>
> And at the same time you can feel the other hand a little heavier on your leg, the feet
> against the floor, the rhythm of your breath — and when you notice the light hand and
> the heavy hand feeling different, you can nod. **[WAIT — nod]**
>
> And it becomes a floating feeling — a tendency to lift with each breath — rising now,
> pivoting, the palm turning gently inward — and I wonder whether it feels more like the
> hand being drawn to the face, or the face to the hand, or both drawn together. I will
> let you decide.

**Key techniques in this passage:**
- "You might imagine" (permissive frame)
- "As if balloons" (inference imagery)
- "You are creating that sensation" (ownership frame)
- "A greater sense of control" (control frame — critical for emotionals)
- "I wonder whether... or... or both" (double bind)
- "I will let you decide" (permission, client-led)

**If the hand stalls:**
- Go with it: "When the hand touches the table, you reach the peak of your suggestibility."
- Or pace: "A tendency to lift with each breath — no rush."
- Never force. Forcing breaks rapport with an emotional client.

**Close:** Skin contact + nod → SNAP → "Deep sleep." → PHS anatomy.

### 3.2 Permissive Eye Fascination

Eye fascination is a secondary induction that layers a second convincer. The emotional
lane withholds closure to build the urge, rather than commanding closure.

> As you keep looking at that spot — the gaze may drift and come back, gently, easily —
> you can begin to notice the small muscles around the eyes, how they hold the eyes up,
> and the eyelids, and what they feel like.
>
> You may begin to blink a little more often. That's it — and the breathing might deepen
> as you relax.
>
> The eyelids may start to feel heavy, and the eyes may want to close. But not just yet —
> let them stay open a little longer.
>
> Many people, entering hypnosis, find the focus going soft — the vision blurring — and
> as you look at that spot it might waver, or blur, or seem to shift shape or color.
>
> And you may notice the arms and legs growing heavy as the eyelids grow heavier and
> heavier, and how good it would feel to let those tired eyes simply relax and close.
>
> When you are ready, you can let them close. **[WAIT — eyes close]**
>
> And closing the eyes means you can relax now — it feels good, peaceful, as you really
> let yourself go.

**Key techniques:**
- "But not just yet" (withholding — builds anticipation and the urge to close)
- "You may begin to blink" (naming what's already happening — pacing)
- "When you are ready" (client-led timing)
- "It might waver, or blur, or seem to shift" (triple bind — any visual change validates)
- "You can relax now" (embedded command within the permissive frame)

### 3.3 The "7 Plus or Minus 2" Overload Induction

George Miller's discovery that short-term memory holds only 7 ± 2 items becomes a
therapeutic tool: overload the conscious mind with sensory tasks until it yields to the
simpler suggestion — relax. This induction is especially effective with analytical or
intellectual emotionals who resist standard relaxation inductions.

**Mechanism:** The therapist stacks sensory awareness tasks one at a time (breathing,
voice, head weight, self-image, music/ambient sound, feet, arms, room temperature,
eye sensation) until the client is tracking 9 inputs simultaneously. Then the therapist
offers a single-point relief: "think only of how relaxed you can be now."

**Structure (original wording):**

> Allow yourself to be as comfortable as you want to be... listening to the sound of my
> voice... and while you're listening, concentrating for a few moments on your breathing...
> breathing slowly and steadily, as though you were sound asleep... and imagining how
> comfortable you might look while relaxing in that chair... using the power of your mind
> to see yourself... and then doing whatever has to happen to make you look even more
> relaxed...

Stack items one by one:
1. Sound of my voice
2. Steadiness of breathing
3. Weight of your head against the chair
4. How you might look from the outside
5. Ambient sounds in the room
6. The way your feet feel on the floor
7. How your arms feel
8. The temperature of the room
9. How your eyes feel

> "And now you can think how good it will feel... when you simply allow yourself to think
> of only the most important thing of all... how relaxed you can be now... relaxing in your
> mind... and in your body... no need to think anything at all... nobody wanting anything
> and nobody expecting anything... and absolutely nothing for you to do except to... relax."

**Key techniques:**
- "I wonder if your mind is powerful enough to think of seven plus one things" (challenge/bind)
- Rapid scanning = timesharing — the conscious mind can't hold all 9
- The relief of collapsing to one focus creates an instant drop
- Works with clients who "think themselves out" of standard inductions

**Practical tip:** Keep a cheat sheet of the 9 items visible. If you lose track, so does the
client — and that's actually fine, because the confusion itself is part of the overload.

### 3.4 Ericksonian Confusion with Levitation

This Nongard-style induction uses ambiguity, left/right confusion, and subconscious
choice to bypass the critical mind. It is pure inferred work — the client decides which
hand is "lighter" and the therapist never commands the lift.

**Structure:**

1. **Frame:** "I'm not going to ask you to do anything uncomfortable. This is a learning
   process — learning the difference between the conscious mind and the subconscious mind."

2. **Hand choice:** "Look at your two hands. You may see some similarities, or perhaps
   you notice differences. Your conscious mind may hear the words I'm using, but the
   subconscious mind will actually make a choice between the two hands."

3. **Left/right bind:** "You may choose the left hand. Perhaps you will find that is the
   *right* choice for you. Or you might choose the right hand — the other hand will be the
   only one, of course, that is *left*." (Linguistic confusion — right/left double meaning)

4. **Lightness:** "Your subconscious mind knows which of those two hands is lighter than
   the other. Your conscious mind may be aware of the choices that your subconscious mind
   has made. Or perhaps it is not. That is fine too."

5. **Balloon:** "It's almost as if a balloon has been tied around the wrist. As the hand
   begins to move gently, that balloon causes the hand to rise."

6. **Deepening close:** "In a moment, I am going to ask you to allow yourself to
   completely and freely experience a moment of tranquility — a moment perhaps more
   peaceful than any other experience you have ever identified."

**Key techniques:**
- Client is never told *which* hand will lift — the subconscious "decides"
- Conscious/subconscious split language keeps the analytical mind busy
- Left/right word play creates Ericksonian confusion
- "Your conscious mind may wonder how this occurs" — normalizes the mystery
- Post-induction anchor: "At any time, if you experience difficulty, you can bring
  yourself back to this point"

### 3.5 Auto Dual Induction (Intellectual Suggestible)

For the **intellectual suggestible** (80%+ emotional, who talks/questions themselves out
of the arm-raise), the auto dual induction uses **self-spoken repeat-after-me** to make
them suggestible to their own voice.

**When to use:** The arm-raise has stalled despite rescue attempts. The client is
analytical, keeps their eyes moving, stays "in their head."

**Mechanism:** Three conscious acceptances (sit up, finger on pulse, stare at fingernail)
prime the yes-pattern. Then the client repeats each phrase aloud after the therapist. The
act of speaking prevents simultaneous analysis — when the therapist reaches zero, the
client is psychologically and physically prepared to follow the final suggestion of deep
sleep, because the suggestion came from their own voice.

**The count:**
1. "I will now enter the state of hypnosis for reasons of deep relaxation and self-control."
2. "I will count from five down to zero and with each count I will become more deeply relaxed."
3. "Five — I begin to feel my breathing growing deep, gentle, and rhythmic."
4. "Four — I begin to feel heaviness in my eyelids, as I become drowsier and sleepier."
5. "Three — I begin to feel every muscle, nerve, and fiber in my body relaxing."
6. "Two — My arms, my legs, my entire body are now deeply relaxed."
7. "One — My eyelids grow heavier, breathing becoming more rhythmic, I am deeply relaxed."
8. "Zero — Deep Sleep!"

**Therapist cues:** Begin suggesting just as you see the natural change: breathing pattern
varies → suggest breathing deepening. Head jerks forward → suggest heaviness. Eyelids
tire from staring at the fingernail → suggest drowsiness. Always ride the natural change,
never command ahead of it.

**Key control-frame words** (post-induction): allowing, because you choose to allow it,
giving yourself permission, a greater sense of control, tendency, peaceful, safe.

See the Auto Dual drill in the Practice Lab for the full step-by-step sequence.

### 3.6 Guided Imagery Induction

A secondary induction built entirely on imagery — the most natural vehicle for
emotional clients.

**Structure:** Breath focus → head-to-toe release → 5-0 count → journey → embedded
therapeutic message → suggestions.

**The Kappasinian leaf metaphor:**
> A single beautiful tree on a hill. Approach and take in its form and the leaf-sounds.
> One leaf at the branch tip twists and turns, breaks free, glides down to rest at the
> base. At the roots, a single blade of grass — persevered and strong through its own
> motivation.
>
> "The same way YOU are motivated" — take what we discussed, understand it, help yourself.
> Carry the feelings forward.

**Key:** Always offer the visualize/imagine/conceptualize fallback. Not everyone sees
pictures — some feel, some hear, some "just know."

## Part 4: Inferred Deepening

### 4.1 Permissive Staircase

The standard staircase deepener adapts to emotional clients by using their own imagery
and multisensory detail rather than bare counting:

> "Picture yourself at the top of a beautiful staircase — or perhaps a garden path, or a
> forest trail — whatever feels right for you."

Each step adds sensory detail:
- Step 10: "The stone is warm beneath your feet."
- Step 9: "You can hear water somewhere nearby."
- Step 8: "The light is softer here, golden."
- Step 7: "The scent of something familiar and comforting."

**Pacing language:** "With each step... drifting... settling... each one a little more
comfortable."

**At the bottom:** "A place of complete comfort and safety — Deep Sleep."

### 4.2 Permissive Progressive Relaxation

The body scan uses permissive language:

| Direct | Inferred |
|---|---|
| "Relax the muscles in your forehead" | "Allow the muscles in your forehead to soften" |
| "Release the tension in your jaw" | "You may notice the jaw beginning to let go" |
| "Your arms are heavy and relaxed" | "The arms might feel pleasantly heavy, or warm, or simply still" |

**Outside-sounds reframe:** "And any sounds from outside this room simply send you deeper
— each one a reminder that you can relax more completely."

### 4.3 Count 5-0 (Permissive)

> "And as I count from five down to zero, you may find yourself drifting a little deeper
> with each number..."
>
> Five... allowing the body to settle...
> Four... perhaps noticing how comfortable you are becoming...
> Three... and with each breath, a little more peaceful...
> Two... drifting now, easily, naturally...
> One... almost there... and...
> Zero — Deep Sleep.

## Part 5: Inferred Suggestions

### 5.1 The Say-See-Feel Arc

Every therapeutic suggestion follows three steps:

1. **Say it:** State the suggestion in permissive language
2. **See it:** Paint the outcome scene in the client's life (their imagery, their details)
3. **Feel it:** Anchor it in the body (warmth, lightness, calm, strength)

**Example (presentation confidence):**

> *Say:* "You might find that when you stand before a group, a calm steadiness settles
> through you — the words arriving easily, your voice clear and sure."
>
> *See:* "Picture yourself in that conference room tomorrow — the faces around the table,
> the light from the windows, your notes in front of you — and notice how your hands rest
> still on the table."
>
> *Feel:* "And feel that steadiness now — in the chest, in the shoulders, in the breath —
> a quiet confidence that belongs to you."

### 5.2 Future-Pacing

Place the suggestion in a specific future scene from the client's actual life:

> "And tomorrow, in that meeting, you might notice how calm your hands feel on the table.
> You may find that the words come more easily than you expected. Perhaps someone will
> notice and say something, and you will simply nod, knowing this is who you have always
> been."

### 5.3 Identity Close

End the suggestion sequence by reframing the change as a **return**, not a transformation:

> "Not becoming someone new — remembering who you have always been."

This is powerful for emotional clients because it validates their identity rather than
implying they were broken.

### 5.4 Permissive Coda

> "No rush — unfolding naturally, with each breath."

This releases time pressure and lets the subconscious integrate at its own pace.

## Part 6: Metaphor Design

### 6.1 Isomorphic Structure

A therapeutic metaphor has a **parallel structure** to the client's situation. It is
not identical (that would engage the critical mind) but analogous — the subconscious
maps the story elements onto the client's own experience.

**Five elements:**
1. **Protagonist** — a character the client can identify with
2. **Challenge** — mirrors the client's presenting issue
3. **Resource** — a quality or ally the protagonist discovers
4. **Transformation** — the shift that resolves the challenge
5. **New normal** — life after the change (normalized, integrated)

### 6.2 Nature Metaphors

| Image | Maps to |
|---|---|
| **Tree** | Roots = foundation/history; trunk = strength; branches = growth/options; leaves = letting go |
| **River** | Flow = life path; rocks = obstacles; the river flows around them; still water = peace |
| **Seed** | Dormant potential; darkness = current struggle; sprouting = emerging change; bloom = full realization |
| **Mountain** | Climb = effort; summit = perspective; vista = seeing the whole picture |

### 6.3 The Kappasinian Leaf & Blade

The guided imagery block includes the canonical leaf metaphor:

> A single leaf at the branch tip. It twists and turns in the breeze. It holds on, then
> lets go — not falling, but *gliding* — carried gently down through the branches, past
> the bark, to rest at the base of the tree.
>
> And there at the roots, a single blade of grass. Small, but it persevered. Through rain
> and wind and the weight of the world above, it grew — strong through its own motivation.
>
> "The same way YOU are motivated."

### 6.4 Delivery

- **Pace the imagery.** Silence between images lets the subconscious illustrate.
- **Multisensory.** Sight + sound + texture + scent + temperature minimum.
- **Leave space.** "And you might see... or perhaps you simply feel..." — don't
  over-specify the client's inner world.
- **Close forward.** "Carry these feelings with you into the days ahead."

## Part 7: Voice & Tonality for Inferred Sessions

### 7.1 The E-Override Rule

When the client is emotional (60%+ emotional suggestibility), certain stages that
normally use **paternal** voice shift to **maternal**:

| Stage | Default Voice | E-Override |
|---|---|---|
| Physiology talk | Paternal | **Maternal** |
| Conversion | Paternal | **Maternal** |
| Eye fascination | Paternal | **Maternal** |
| Heavy/light | Paternal | **Maternal** |
| Finger spread | Paternal | **Maternal** |
| Reactional | Authority | **Paternal** (softer, not full authority) |

### 7.2 Tone Parameters

| Tonality | Rate | Pitch | Volume | Pace | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Maternal** | -5% | +1 | Soft | 0.92 | Inferred induction, permissive deepening |
| **Theta hypnotic** | -10% | -4 | Soft | 0.82 | Suggestions, deep trance imagery |
| **Conversational** | +3% | 0 | Medium | 1.0 | Pre-talk, self-hypnosis teach |
| **Paternal** | 0% | -4 | Medium | 1.0 | E-override for reactional |
| **Authority** | +5% | -3 | Medium | 1.05 | Count-out only |

### 7.3 Pause Multiplier

Emotional clients benefit from a **1.3x pause multiplier** (compared to 1.0 for
physical). Every scripted pause is 30% longer. This gives the subconscious more
processing time and communicates patience — which emotional clients interpret as
respect.

### 7.4 Analog Marking

When delivering an embedded command within a permissive sentence:
1. Micro-pause before the command (0.3-0.5s)
2. Drop pitch 5-10%
3. Slow the rate slightly
4. Deliver the command with quiet certainty
5. Micro-pause after (0.3s)
6. Resume the permissive wrapper at normal pace

The listener's conscious mind hears a flowing sentence. The subconscious registers the
marked phrase as a directive.

## Part 8: Practice Drills

### Drill 1: Inferred Rapport (2-3 min)

**Focus:** Mirror, pace, lead with permissive language.

Checklist:
- [ ] Breathing rhythm matched before the first verbal lead
- [ ] At least two body-language channels mirrored
- [ ] Three verifiable truisms paced before leading
- [ ] Permissive language throughout (no "you will" commands)
- [ ] Client's own predicates used in at least one lead
- [ ] Emotional axis held (understood, not instructed)
- [ ] Clean bridge into the induction

### Drill 2: Inferred Arm-Raising Induction (4-6 min)

**Focus:** Permissive conversion with ownership and double binds.

Checklist:
- [ ] Permissive framing throughout (no direct "it IS lifting" commands)
- [ ] Balloon/helium imagery used for lightness
- [ ] Ownership frame delivered ("you are creating")
- [ ] Double bind offered (hand-to-face / face-to-hand / both)
- [ ] Misdirection woven with heavy-hand imagery
- [ ] Stalling handled with go-with reframe (not forced)
- [ ] Closure withheld until client initiates
- [ ] Skin contact → snap → deep sleep → PHS

### Drill 3: Inferred Deepening (3-4 min)

**Focus:** Permissive eye fascination + sensory staircase.

Checklist:
- [ ] Eye fascination ran permissive (withhold closure, not command-close)
- [ ] Blink-heavy loop observed and paced (not rushed)
- [ ] Vision-blur suggestion offered as possibility
- [ ] Close initiated by the client (not commanded)
- [ ] Staircase scene chosen by the client's imagery
- [ ] Sensory detail on at least three steps
- [ ] Bottom anchored to comfort + deep sleep + PHS

### Drill 4: Inferred Suggestions (3-4 min)

**Focus:** Permissive openers, presuppositions, embedded commands, future-pacing.

Checklist:
- [ ] At least two permissive openers used (might/perhaps/may)
- [ ] At least one presupposition delivered smoothly
- [ ] Embedded command marked with voice drop and slight pause
- [ ] Truism chain (3 truths + suggestion) delivered
- [ ] Say-See-Feel arc complete for the main suggestion
- [ ] Future-pace placed in a specific scene from their life
- [ ] Suggestions used the client's own keywords
- [ ] Unhurried delivery with the permissive coda

### Drill 5: Metaphor & Guided Imagery Design (2-3 min)

**Focus:** Build an isomorphic metaphor with multisensory detail.

Checklist:
- [ ] Metaphor was isomorphic to the client's situation
- [ ] All five elements present (protagonist, challenge, resource, transformation, new normal)
- [ ] At least three senses engaged (visual + two others)
- [ ] Therapeutic message embedded within the narrative
- [ ] Pacing allowed silence between key images
- [ ] Metaphor resolution mirrored the client's desired outcome
- [ ] Closing carried the feelings forward

- Voice drop + micro-pause before/after
