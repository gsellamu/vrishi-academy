TEACH Self-Hypnosis — The 4-Step Method (20 min)

Alright. Self-hypnosis has four steps. I'm going to explain each one, then we'll practice together. Ready?

Step 1: Eye Fixation + Breathing

This is the entry point. You pick a spot on the wall or ceiling — slightly above your natural eye line, so your eyes have to look up just a little. That slight upward gaze naturally fatigues the eye muscles and signals the brain that it's time to shift states.

Focus on that spot. Take three slow breaths. In through the nose, out through the mouth. Make the exhale about three times longer than the inhale. That ratio activates your parasympathetic nervous system — it's the body's natural calm switch.

On the third exhale, let your eyes close. Don't force them. Just let them fall shut when they're ready. That's your entry.

Step 2: Progressive Relaxation

This is the same body scan we've done in every session. You already know it. Starting from the top of your head, your attention moves down through your body — forehead, jaw, neck, shoulders, arms, chest, stomach, legs, feet. As your attention touches each area, it relaxes. Like warm water pouring through you.

The key: don't rush. Take about 60 to 90 seconds. By the time you reach your feet, your whole body should feel heavy and still. If any area is tight, stay there an extra breath. Then move on.

Step 3: Staircase + Safety Sphere

In your mind, see the staircase. Count down from 10 to 1. With each step, you go deeper. At the bottom, there's your safety sphere. Step inside. Feel the transparent walls. See the world outside. You're safe. This is your workspace. Everything that happens from here reaches your subconscious.

When you do this on your own, you don't need to build the sphere from scratch every time. It's already there. It was built in session. You're just re-entering it. Like going back to your apartment — you don't rebuild the walls every time you come home. You just walk in.

Step 4: Anchor + Suggestion

Place your hand on your heart. Take a deep breath. Say your anchor word silently. Feel the feeling it brings. Then give yourself one suggestion — whatever you need most today. It has to be positive. What you want, not what you don't want. "I am grounded." "I deserve to be seen." "Change is growth." Whatever fits. Repeat it three times. Then count yourself back up: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — eyes open.

That's it. Four steps. Five minutes. Every night before sleep.

Important things to know:
  • This is NOT meditation. Meditation is open-ended awareness. Self-hypnosis has structure: induction, deepening, suggestion, emergence. The structure is what makes it work.
  • The suggestion must be positive. Your subconscious can't process negatives. "Don't be anxious" becomes a picture of anxiety. "I am calm and grounded" gives it something to build.
  • Five minutes daily is better than thirty minutes once a week. Consistency creates the pathway.
  • It will feel lighter than when I guide you. That's normal. You're not doing it wrong. The depth increases with practice. By week three, you'll go deeper on your own than you do with me.
  • If your mind wanders, don't fight it. Just gently come back to the staircase. Wandering is normal. It's not failure.

Any questions before we try it?

Answer questions. Common ones: "How do I know if I'm deep enough?" (If you're relaxed and focused, you're deep enough. Don't judge the depth — just do the steps.) "What if I fall asleep?" (That's fine. The suggestion lands anyway. Your subconscious doesn't sleep when your body does.)
