SESSION-WIDE CLINICAL RULES:
• Emotional lane throughout: permissive, imagery-rich. "You may notice," "allow," "at your own pace." No direct commands.
• No regression. Adult perspective ALWAYS. New safe environment.
• If abreaction: deepen trance, guide to safe space, grounding language. Do NOT count out abruptly.
• Permission language: "with your consent," "only as comfortable."
• Exit signal: right index finger raise = immediate pause/stop.
• Agency language for re-parenting: client discovers what to say, not therapist.
• No negatives in suggestions (Lisa): "I am safe" not "I am not afraid."
• Record from Step 4 through Step 11.
• Evidence-based safeguards: memories in trance may be symbolic, not factual. Do not confirm or dismiss uncertain material.
• Do not interpret images or feelings as literal memories. Approach uncertain material without confirmation or dismissal.

STEP 2: PRIMARY WOUND IDENTIFICATION — LISA'S METHOD (10 min)

T.M., I want to share something with you before we go into the deeper work tonight. There are three primary wounds that tend to sit underneath everything else. These are not diagnoses. They are patterns. Most people who carry unresolved experiences from childhood can relate to one of them more than the others. Sometimes two. Sometimes all three to different degrees.

I am going to describe each one, and I want you to just listen. Do not analyze. Do not try to pick the right answer. Just notice which one lands. Which one your body reacts to. Which one feels most true.

Wound 1: Abandonment

The first one is abandonment. This is the feeling that the people who were supposed to keep you safe could not be present. They were not there in the way you needed them to be. And the message that gets encoded is: "I was not important enough for them to stay. I was not worth being present for." It does not mean they literally left. It means the care, the attention, the protection that should have been there was missing. And you felt alone in moments when you should not have been alone.

Wound 2: Betrayal

The second one is betrayal. This is the feeling that the people who were supposed to keep you safe hurt you instead. Through their words, their actions, their choices. They were supposed to be the ones who protected you, and instead they became the source of the pain. Hitting. Yelling. Intimidating. Sacrificing your safety for their needs. The message that gets encoded is: "The people closest to me are dangerous. If they can hurt me, anyone can."

Wound 3: Shame

The third one is shame. This is the feeling that you were not who your parents or family wanted you to be. You were not the right kind of kid. Not tough enough, not quiet enough, not smart enough, not the right way. And the message that gets encoded is: "There is something fundamentally wrong with who I am. I arrived broken." It does not have to be something they said directly. It can be in how they looked at you, what they praised in others, what they criticized in you.

Identification

Take a moment. Which of those three feels most true for you? Which one lands the hardest? There is no wrong answer. And it can be more than one.

[PAUSE. Give him genuine time. Do not fill the silence. Watch his face, his body. Where does tension appear? Which description made him shift in his seat, clench his jaw, look away, or get quiet? His body already answered before his words do.]
[RECORD which wound(s) he identifies. Clinical expectation based on intake: likely BETRAYAL (family hurt him, mother was a source of harm) + ABANDONMENT (felt abandoned, alone, not protected). Possibly SHAME (not comfortable in own skin). Do NOT lead him. Let him choose. His answer determines the wound-specific language in Steps 9 and 10.]

Thank you for being honest about that. That takes courage to name.

IF HE IDENTIFIES ONE WOUND: "Good. That is clear. We are going to work with that tonight."
IF HE IDENTIFIES TWO: "Both of those make sense given what you have shared. Which one feels like the deeper one? The one underneath the other? We will start there tonight. We will address the second one in a future session."
IF HE IDENTIFIES ALL THREE: "That is not uncommon. When the core experience was significant, all three can be present. Which one is loudest right now? Which one runs the most of your daily life? We will start there."
IF HE IS UNSURE: "That is okay. Sometimes the body knows before the mind does. Let me ask it differently. When you think about what happened to you as a child, what is the first feeling that comes up? Is it being alone? Being hurt? Or being rejected for who you are?"
