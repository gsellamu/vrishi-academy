STEP 6: THEORY OF MIND (5-7 min)

The Circle Model (conscious / critical mind / subconscious)

[Draw the circle on paper or whiteboard as you explain. Visual learners and kinesthetic processors both benefit from seeing the model. Point to each layer as you describe it. This is not optional — the visual anchor dramatically increases comprehension and buy-in.]

Let me show you how your mind is organized. I am going to draw this out so you can see it.

Picture a circle. At the very bottom is the primitive area. This is the oldest part of your brain. It has been there since before humans could speak. Its job is survival: fight, flight, or freeze. It makes decisions in milliseconds, faster than your conscious mind can think. It is the part that flinches before you know why. And for someone who experienced difficult things as a child, this part got very well-trained very early. It learned to stay alert, to watch for danger, to keep the guard up. That was intelligent. That kept you safe.

Above the primitive area is the subconscious mind. This is roughly 88 percent of your total processing power. Everything you have ever experienced is stored here. Your habits, your emotional patterns, your automatic reactions, your core beliefs about yourself and the world. Most of this programming was installed between the ages of zero and eight, when the mind was wide open. No filter. Whatever came in, got recorded. Positive associations, negative associations, all of it. That early programming is still running right now.

Between the subconscious and the outside world is a filter called the critical mind. Think of it as a gatekeeper. Its job is to compare every new idea against what is already stored in the subconscious. If the new idea matches the existing programming, it lets it through. If the new idea contradicts the programming, the critical mind blocks it. That is why willpower alone usually does not work for deep change. Your conscious mind says, "I want to trust people." But the critical mind checks the programming and finds, "Trust equals danger." So it blocks the change. Not because you are weak. Because the system is doing its job.

The top layer is the conscious mind. This is about 12 percent. Logic, reasoning, analysis, decision-making, willpower. The one that does not always work. Your conscious mind can analyze a problem perfectly. It can understand exactly why you react the way you do. But understanding alone does not change the programming. That is what you experienced in therapy — genuine understanding, genuine insight, but the underlying reactions still firing.

[Kappas: focus on behavior, not the story. The goal here is to explain the mechanism of change, not analyze the content of his trauma. Keep the explanation focused on how the system works, not what his specific programming contains.]

How Change Works (and why hypnosis matters)

Here is how a normal thought moves through the system. You have a thought in your conscious mind: "I want to feel safe around people." That thought travels down and hits the critical mind. The critical mind checks the database: "Is safety around people consistent with what is already stored?" If the stored programming says, "People are dangerous," the critical mind blocks the thought. Your conscious intention never reaches the subconscious where the actual change would need to happen.

What hypnosis does is temporarily relax that critical mind filter. Not destroy it. Not override it. Relax it. Just enough that new, healthier associations can reach the subconscious directly. We update the programming at the source. Instead of fighting against the 88 percent with the 12 percent, we get them aligned. Working together.

I work with the 88 percent. You work with the 12 percent. Together, we have 100 percent. That is how real change happens.

The Nervous System Frame (neuroscience grounding)

[Nasir: use neuroscience framing for clients who respond to evidence-based explanation. T.M. has psychotherapy background — he likely understands fight/flight/freeze. Build on existing knowledge rather than starting from scratch.]

Here is another way to understand what is happening. Your nervous system has a threat detection center — the amygdala. When you were a child and things were not safe, that system went on high alert. It learned to scan for danger constantly. That was intelligent adaptation. Your brain did exactly what it was supposed to do to protect you.

The challenge is that it never fully recalibrated. You are 22 now. Your circumstances have changed. But the detection system is still running at the sensitivity level it was set to years ago. That is not a character flaw. That is not weakness. That is a protective program that did its job and has not received the update that the situation has changed.

The vagus nerve connects your brain to your body. When the amygdala fires, the vagus nerve tells your body: speed up the heart, tighten the muscles, flood with cortisol, get ready. That is the hypervigilance you described. The body is responding to a signal that was accurate once but is not accurate now.

Part of what we are going to do is help that system learn that it can adjust the sensitivity. Not turn off. You still need appropriate awareness. But right now the dial is set very high. We want to bring it to a level that matches your actual circumstances today.

Does that resonate with your experience?
