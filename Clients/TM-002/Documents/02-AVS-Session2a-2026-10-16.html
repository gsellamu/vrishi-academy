What We Covered

This was our first hypnotherapy session (the deeper work). We accomplished five things:
  1. We explored a triggering situation from your week: You shared a workplace experience where a customer's behavior triggered internal judgment, followed by guilt about that judgment. We used this as a window into understanding how your mind processes conflict and self-assessment.
  2. Separating identity from behavior: A core principle we established: you are not your reactions. Emotions are situational responses to specific inputs — they do not define who you are. Labeling yourself based on a momentary reaction damages your self-esteem unnecessarily. Like an actor playing different roles, none of those roles is the core you.
  3. Understanding emotional triggers: We identified patterns in how you respond to perceived disrespect and how those responses connect to earlier life experiences. We discussed desensitization — not becoming numb, but reducing how much a trigger affects your inner sense of self so your responses become conscious choices rather than automatic reactions.
  4. Hypnotherapy induction + tribe circle visualization: I guided you into a deeply relaxed state using breathing, affirmations, and a countdown technique. In that deep state, you built a circle of 11 supportive figures around you — your "soul tribe" — each representing a quality you need (bravery, care, self-esteem, belonging, and others). These were integrated into your body and sealed with protective energy.
  5. Anchor installation: We installed a physical anchor — pressing your thumb and forefinger together on your right hand — that instantly returns you to the integrated, protected, self-assured state from the visualization. You can use this anytime you need it.

Key Concepts from This Session

Identity vs. behavior: Your reactions are not your identity. They are contextual, triggered by specific situations. You can observe them, understand them, and change them — without judging yourself for having them.
Projection: How we judge others often reflects our own inner wounds and fears. As those wounds heal, judgment of others naturally softens.
Desensitization: Reducing how much a trigger affects your ego and self-esteem, so your responses become chosen rather than automatic. Not numb — responsive and engaged, without the inner self being breached.
The rescuer in you: You have a natural capacity for bravery and care (the river rescue, the beach memory). When someone is rude or angry, you can reframe it as them struggling rather than them attacking you. This shifts you from defender to helper — a much more powerful position.
